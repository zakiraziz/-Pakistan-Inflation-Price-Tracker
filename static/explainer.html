The headline

The equal-weight basket of 11 everyday goods rose about +114% in PKR from January 2023 to late 2026 — an average of roughly 24% per year. A shopper who needed Rs 6,477 to fill the basket in early 2023 needs about Rs 13,860 to buy the same things today. Prices broadly doubled in three and a half years.

Why the lines are steep in 2023 and flatten by 2025–26

Zoom into 2023–24 and the lines climb steeply; from 2025 on they are much flatter. This mirrors Pakistan's actual inflation narrative:

  1. 2022–23 — price shocks stack. The 2022 floods hit cotton, rice, onion and potato harvests; the rupee depreciated against the dollar (raising the PKR cost of imported oil and food inputs); and electricity tariffs were re-based. Food and energy prices ratcheted up together — hence the steep start of every line.
  2. 2024 — high but easing. Headline inflation stayed in double digits but the pace slowed as shock effects left the year-on-year comparison and policy tightened.
  3. 2025–26 — disinflation. With the price base already ~doubled, new percentage increases shrank even though prices stayed elevated. Flat lines here mean falling inflation, not falling prices.

Key concept: inflation is the rate of change of prices, not the price level. A big initial shock pushes both the level and the y/y reading up for about a year (the "base effect"); as that base rolls off, the same high level can coexist with low inflation.

Energy rises most and pulls the rest up

Electricity (+151%) and petrol (+135%) grew fastest. Energy sits behind almost everything: fuel transports, stores and processes food, and electricity runs mills and retail cold chains. When energy spikes it ripples through the whole basket — why even normally-priced food rose ~100%. That is a classic cost-push (supply-side) channel.

Why the alerts cluster on onion and potato

With the date filters set to one week you'll see the jump alerts fire on fresh produce again and again (e.g. potatoes +36% in a week). These spikes reflect supply and season, not a re-acceleration of general inflation:

  • Harvest timing — plenty right after harvest, scarce months later.
  • Perishability — little buffer between a bad yield and a price spike.
  • Import dependence in lean months — exposing prices to the exchange rate and border logistics.

So a +30% potato week is a scarcity signal, while steady risers like atta, sugar and rice carry the underlying (sticky) inflation burden. Economists separate the volatile food-and-energy part from a "core" measure to judge whether the trend is genuinely cooling — and on this data it is by 2025–26.

This is a representative educational dataset: the shape mirrors Pakistan's real CPI experience (high inflation easing to disinflation, volatile fresh produce, high energy pass-through) rather than being a precise official series.
