What this tracker measures a cost-of-living basket, not a national CPI

The tracker follows a small, fixed basket of goods that dominate household budgets in Pakistan: staples (wheat flour, sugar, rice, cooking oil, ghee, lentils, besan, tea), fresh produce (onion, potato, tomato, garlic, ginger, banana, apple), protein (chicken, eggs, milk, yogurt, beef, mutton) and energy (petrol, diesel, LPG, electricity). Each item is priced in the unit households actually buy, and every observation is stored with its own provenance.

This is a representative educational sample generated deterministically for demonstration. It is not official CPI data and must not be used for policy, indexation or settlement decisions.

Weekly series and seasonality one observation per item per week

Prices are recorded on a fixed seven-day grid anchored at 2023-01-01, so any two series can be compared week by week without interpolation. The underlying growth path decays over time to mirror Pakistan's actual disinflation: roughly 3.3% per month at the start of 2023 easing towards 0.5–0.7% per month by 2026. Each item applies its own growth multiplier, and fresh produce additionally carries a seasonal swing, which is why onion and potato are the most volatile lines in the basket.

The basket cost index equal-weighted, re-based to the selected window

The index is an equal-weighted sum of item prices. On any date, the basket cost is the sum of the 28 item prices for that week. The index on a date equals the basket cost on that date divided by the basket cost on the first date of the selected window, multiplied by 100. The first point of every window is therefore always 100, and the last point shows the percentage change in the cost of the basket over that window. Because it is equal-weighted, a kilogram of onion carries the same influence as a 20 kg bag of flour — the index measures how a household basket behaves, not a national expenditure-weighted price level.

Inflation statistics weekly, annualised and year-on-year

Three headline numbers are derived from the same series. The weekly average change is the geometric mean week-on-week movement across the window. The annualised rate extrapolates the total basket movement over the window to a 365-day rate. The year-on-year rate compares the latest basket cost with the basket cost for the closest week at least 364 days earlier. All three are computed from approved observations only.

Price-jump alerts week-on-week change at or above the threshold

For every item, the most recent approved week is compared with the week before it. If the change is at or above the alert threshold — 5% by default, adjustable in the control bar — the item is flagged with its previous price, new price and percentage change. Alerts are recomputed whenever new data is ingested, and only approved observations can raise an alert, so a price still awaiting review can never appear as a signal.

Provenance and approval every point is attributable and reviewable

Each observation stores its source, collection method and collection timestamp alongside the price. Observations arrive with a status: approved points feed the public series, index, statistics and alerts, while pending points are held back for review and rejected points are permanently excluded from the index. Reviews are recorded through the admin endpoints, so the audit trail explains why a price is or is not in the published series.
